# MIRROR//AI — Project Roadmap

## 1. Product Mission

MIRROR//AI is a playable AI life simulation where players experience how their decisions can shape simulated future outcomes.

Core philosophy:

> **You don't choose your future. You build it through your decisions.**

The hackathon version focuses on one polished vertical slice rather than attempting to build the entire long-term product.

# 6. Final Demo Flow

## 0:00 — Cinematic Intro

Introduce MIRROR//AI.

## 0:20 — Voice Goal

Player speaks:

> "I want financial independence, but family is extremely important."

## 0:40 — AI Profile

The system understands the player's goals and priorities.

## 1:00 — Personalized Scenario

Example:

> A startup offers a high-growth opportunity, but the workload could reduce family time.

## 1:00–2:00 — Meaningful Decisions

Player chooses among meaningful trade-offs.

Example:

```text
A — Take the startup opportunity
B — Stay with the stable career
C — Negotiate a flexible arrangement
```

## 2:00–2:30 — Consequences

The deterministic game engine changes:

* Career
* Money
* Relationships
* Energy
* Stress

## 2:30–3:00 — Time Jump

The game advances through time.

Previous decisions influence future scenarios.

## 3:00 — Future Self Reveal

A Future Self appears in the Babylon.js environment.

## 3:00–3:40 — Conversation

Player asks:

> "Was it worth it?"

Future Self answers using actual gameplay history.

## 3:40–4:20 — What-If

Player asks:

> "What if I had chosen the stable job?"

The system generates an alternate simulated future.

## 4:20–4:40 — Closing

> **MIRROR//AI doesn't predict your future. It lets you experience the consequences of your decisions.**

# 7. Demo Success Criteria

The demo is successful if a judge can clearly see:

```text
Real-world input
       ↓
AI understanding
       ↓
Personalized gameplay
       ↓
Meaningful decision
       ↓
Real consequence
       ↓
Memory
       ↓
Time progression
       ↓
Future Self
       ↓
What-If
```

The judge should understand that removing the AI would materially change the experience.

The real-world input must also materially influence the gameplay.

# 8. Freeze Rule

At approximately 18 hours:

> **Feature freeze.**

Only:

* Bug fixes
* Integration fixes
* UI polish
* Demo preparation
* Reliability improvements

No new major architecture.

No unnecessary refactoring.

No new experimental systems unless required to make the demo work.
